# Stopgap stress test

Reviewed 2026-10-20. Scope is this idea only: a drafting agent that, for a US drug shortage, assembles a pharmacist-signed substitution memo from free government data. No replacement product is proposed. No drug is named here as a safe substitute. Figures below are from pages or API responses opened on this date unless a line says the source returned an error or only a search-index extract.

## Do the demand statistics match the sources?

### Takeaway

The openFDA "1,141 current" figure is real and dated 2026-10-16, but 741 of those records are marked Available, so it is the wrong crisis number. The Vizient labor story is real for calendar year 2023 and the exact $894 million / 24.2 / 35.4 figures appear in a secondary write-up, not in Vizient's own release. The ASHP "227" figure matches a June 2026 statistics page extract, but that extract says the rise is the second quarter in a row, not the third, and the cited Becker's article returned HTTP 403. The 32% ASHP survey figure is the share who called shortages "critical," with that word defined as rationing, delay, or cancellation. The 78.3% Bluesight figure matches the company's own June 2026 release; "shortage software is a top-5 tech initiative" was not in that release.

### Inferences

- A pitch that says "1,141 current shortages" will be marked wrong by anyone who filters on availability. The defensible openFDA sentence is: 1,141 status=Current presentation records as of 2026-10-16, of which 270 are Unavailable and 129 are Limited Availability.
- Dopamine 40 mg/mL is a bad demo of therapeutic substitution. The shortage file itself shows other manufacturers' 40 mg/mL vials as Available. The pharmacist job on this record is same-ingredient sourcing, and one Hikma presentation's own related_info points at resupply during October 2026.
- "Third straight quarterly rise" should be dropped or rewritten to the ASHP page's "second quarter in a row" until Becker's can be read. Quoting 227 as if it were the October 20, 2026 live count overreaches a June 2026 series.
- The labor claim is usable if stated the way Vizient stated it (2023, roughly 20 million hours, nearly $900 million, up from just under $360 million in 2019, 132-respondent survey). The 24.2 and 35.4 hour figures are usable only with the Periop Leader citation, not as if they were on the news release.

### Gaps

- Becker's body was not read (HTTP 403). Whether Becker's itself says "third" or misstated ASHP is unknown.
- The ASHP statistics page and the ASHP dopamine bulletin both returned HTTP 403, so the June 2026 wording is from a search-index extract, not a full page read. No later ASHP quarterly number was verified from an opened ASHP page.
- The full Vizient report PDF was not opened, so $894 million versus "nearly $900 million," and 20 million versus 20.2 million, were not checked against the report tables. A Becker's secondary snippet that places the $894 million and the hours in 2024 rather than 2023 was not opened and is not used as a correction.
- The HPOR top-five ranking was not confirmed by a local PDF parse.
- The ASHP 2023 survey PDF linked from PR Newswire was not opened. A 42% "delayed or canceled treatments" management-strategy figure appears in secondary coverage and was not verified here.

## Can the named free datasets be joined on a real shortage drug this week?

### Takeaway

On the Hikma dopamine NDC, openFDA joins to RxNav and DailyMed on identifiers that were returned live. Orange Book joins on the ingredient name and returns pharmaceutical-equivalence records, including discontinued ones, not therapeutic substitutes. NADAC returned no row for that NDC. The VA National Formulary catalog page does not contain a drug table, and the linked VA page had no dopamine string. ASHP pages returned HTTP 403 from this client even though robots.txt does not disallow them. A weekend prototype can draft a sourced availability packet for this NDC. It cannot price this NDC from NADAC, and it cannot honestly treat RxClass or the Orange Book as a substitute list.

### Inferences

## Does anyone already do shortage tracking, alternative suggestion, or substitution memos?

### Takeaway

The claim "nobody ships an agent that does the substitution reasoning with evidence" is false as an absolute and unproven in the narrow form "no commercial product emits a citation-backed memo for a pharmacist to sign." Shortage tracking and supply substitution are crowded. A public MCP already joins openFDA to RxClass and rechecks whether the class sibling is short. MaaTRx's university announcement says the product identifies safe alternatives and substitution guidance. Bluesight already sells shortage software and tells buyers to stock-check therapeutic alternatives. What was not found, on the pages opened, is a product that writes the signed memo. Failure to find that page is not proof it does not exist. Wolters Kluwer, UpToDate, Lexidrug, Medi-Span, McKesson, and Oracle Health product pages were not opened.

Job it does: manufacturer-reported current and resolved shortages and discontinuations. The April 2, 2026 guide, opened, says ShortageCheck monitors market-wide signals across acute care and combines them with a hospital's inventory so teams are told whether a predicted shortage is a problem for their supply, and are given tools to plan a response. It says platforms of this type flag risk 30–90 days ahead of formal listings, and that no tool eliminates false positives. The GitHub repo Travis-Clement-Dev/rx-shortage-mcp, opened, is an MCP server "that finds same-class therapeutic alternatives for a drug in shortage and flags which alternatives are also short," built on openFDA plus RxNorm/RxClass. Job it does: normalize a drug, fetch a class, list siblings, recheck shortage status, and hand that to an LLM. Job it does not do: a price, a hospital formulary check, or a signed memo. Its existence means "an agent over openFDA and RxClass" is already public, and its class-sibling step is the unsafe shortcut a judge can recognize. — [rx-shortage-mcp](https://github.com/Travis-Clement-Dev/rx-shortage-mcp)

### Inferences

- Mark the proposer's "nobody" sentence false if it means no one assembles shortage status with class-level alternatives. Mark the narrower sentence, "no commercial product ships a pharmacist-signed, citation-backed substitution memo," as unverified, not as a finding of white space. The demo has to show a job those opened pages do not claim, or it looks like a wrapper on openFDA plus RxClass, which already exists as a public MCP, next to Bluesight and Vizient products hospitals already buy.
- The buyer Bluesight measured is already being sold prediction and inventory tools. Adoption of dedicated shortage software is 16.5% against 69.7% spreadsheets, so a drafting aid can still be aimed at the spreadsheet majority, but only if it does not pretend to be the first shortage product.

### Gaps

- No Wolters Kluwer, UpToDate, Lexidrug, Medi-Span, McKesson, or Oracle Health page was opened. Any of those could already sell therapeutic alternatives or interchange content inside an EHR or wholesaler workflow.
- No current Epic product page was opened. Older public descriptions of Willow therapeutic-interchange builds were not used as evidence of what ships in 2026.
- ASHP bulletin "alternative agents" sections were not read (HTTP 403).
- MaaTRx's actual UI was not seen. The university article's "safe alternatives" and "clinically appropriate substitutions" language is a claim about the product, not a demonstration that the reasoning is evidence-linked.
- Search was not a census of every 2024–2026 startup. Absence of a memo product is not proof of absence.

## What clinical and legal constraints change the demo?

### Takeaway

The memo cannot say a drug is a safe substitute. openFDA forbids relying on its data for medical-care decisions. State generic substitution is not therapeutic interchange. A P&T committee, not an agent, is what authorizes a different drug. The VA National Formulary is a VA must-stock list, not this hospital's protocol. The demo drug's own file shows same-strength product still reported Available, so a therapeutic-interchange memo would be the wrong document.

### Cited Findings

- openFDA's own response text requires this limitation: "Do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care" and "you should assume all results are unvalidated." The license extract says not to imply endorsement. A memo that treats an openFDA row as proof a substitute is safe contradicts the source. The disclaimer has to be visible on the artifact, not buried. — [Live shortages API meta](https://api.fda.gov/drug/shortages.json?limit=1); [openFDA license](https://open.fda.gov/license/)
- RxNav's terms require the NLM attribution sentence quoted in the data-source section, bar use of the NLM name and logo as an endorsement, and say NLM does not provide specific medical advice. RxClass adds the SNOMED CT affiliate-license condition. A class name returned by RxClass is not permission to interchange. — [RxNav terms](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/TermsofService.html)
- The FDA Clinical Decision Support Software FAQ page returned HTTP 200. Its visible text is a graphic overview of the CDS guidance. The four non-device CDS criteria were not present as extractable text on that page, so this review does not treat device-status as settled from that URL. — [FDA CDS FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs)
- What a substitution memo is allowed to claim, from the sources above rather than from a statute page: it can quote shortage fields (status, availability, reason, dates, NDC, company, related_info), quote a label by setid and published date, and show that another presentation of the same ingredient is reported Available or Unavailable. It cannot conclude that a different molecule is safe, equivalent, or appropriate for a patient. The 2023 ASHP survey release says offering therapeutic alternatives was a strategy 97% of respondents used, which shows the human task is real. It does not authorize software to pick the alternative. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-ashp-survey-critical-shortages-of-cancer-drugs-pose-dangers-to-patient-care-301898041.html)
- Generic substitution and therapeutic interchange are not the same, and this demo drug shows why. Orange Book records opened for dopamine hydrochloride are same-ingredient products with a marketing status, strength, dosage form, and route. Pfizer's 40 mg/1 mL presentations are already marked Available on the shortage file. Switching among those is a sourcing or pharmaceutical-equivalence question. Switching to a different drug would be therapeutic interchange, which hospitals handle under a P&T-approved protocol rather than under a state's generic-substitution statute. No state statute page was opened for this review, so the state-by-state rules are a gap. The operational distinction is still forced by the records that were opened: same-ingredient availability and Orange Book status are in the data; a "safe substitute" conclusion is not. — [FDA dopamine shortage page](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/drugshortages/dsp_ActiveIngredientDetails.cfm?AI=Dopamine+Hydrochloride+Injection&st=c); [Orange Book API result described above](https://api.fda.gov/drug/orangebook.json?search=products.active_ingredients.name:%22DOPAMINE+HYDROCHLORIDE%22&limit=1)
- VA National Formulary as "our hospital" is not honest. The catalog description limits it to products that must be stocked at VA facilities and that VISNs and VA medical centers cannot make non-formulary. It is not a community hospital P&T protocol, and the page opened did not show a dopamine row. CC0 covers reuse of the listing. It does not turn the listing into clinical interchange authority. — [VA National Formulary catalog record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/va-national-formulary)
- Disclaimer language the opened sources actually require on the artifact: the openFDA "do not rely" sentence; the assumption that results are unvalidated; no implication of FDA endorsement; the NLM attribution sentence if RxNorm or RxClass is used; no NLM logo; and a line that the pharmacist, not the software, decides. DailyMed's web-services page did not add a further clinical sentence.

### Inferences

- The winning shape of the memo is an evidence packet with an empty clinical conclusion: shortage facts, same-ingredient availability, label citations, price present or explicitly missing, and a signature block. The losing shape is a sentence that names another drug as safe.
- Because one Hikma 40 mg/mL presentation's related_info points at October 2026 resupply, a memo dated this week that skips the same-ingredient rows and jumps to a different drug is the error a pharmacist judge is paid to catch.
- P&T is the load-bearing user, not a footer. The agent drafts for a committee or for a pharmacist who already has a protocol. It does not create the protocol.

### Gaps

- No state pharmacy act or board rule was opened, so the review cannot say which states allow therapeutic interchange, require prescriber notification, or restrict narrow-therapeutic-index substitution.
- FDA CDS device versus non-device status was not determined from the FAQ page's graphic. Whether a signed drafting tool is a device is a legal gap, not a cleared path.
- ASHP's own guideline on therapeutic interchange was not opened.
- Full DailyMed sections, and any label disclaimer, were not quoted.

## What would make this idea more likely to win, and what would sink it?

### Takeaway

Keep the product, change the demo. The buildable, verified job is a sourced packet that shows this Hikma presentation unavailable while other 40 mg/mL presentations are reported available, with label and class identifiers attached and no clinical conclusion. The idea loses if it quotes 1,141 as the crisis, calls a class sibling a safe substitute, invents a NADAC price, pretends the VA list is the hospital, or looks like the public openFDA-plus-RxClass MCP with a memo template on top.

### Cited Findings

- Weakness already measured: of 1,141 status=Current records, 741 are Available and 270 are Unavailable. A demo that starts from status=Current alone will "substitute" drugs the file says are available. — [openFDA count=availability](https://api.fda.gov/drug/shortages.json?count=availability)
- Weakness already measured on the proposed drug: Hikma NDC 0143-9254-25 and 0143-9252-25 are Unavailable for demand increase, while Pfizer NDC 0409-5820-01 and 0409-9104-20 at 40 mg/1 mL are Available, updated 10/08/2026. NDC 0143-9254-25's related_info points at October 2026. — [openFDA dopamine records](https://api.fda.gov/drug/shortages.json?search=generic_name:dopamine&limit=100)
- Weakness already measured on price: both NDC normalizations of 0143-9254-25 returned zero NADAC rows from dataset `fbb83258-11c7-47f5-8b18-5f8e79f7e704`. — [NADAC 2026 dataset](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/fbb83258-11c7-47f5-8b18-5f8e79f7e704)
- Weakness already measured on class data: RxClass returned EPC "Catecholamine" for rxcui 1743938. That is not a substitute list. — [RxNav terms and the live class call](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/RxNav/TermsofService.html)
- Weakness already measured on competition and buyer behavior: Bluesight says 69.7% of 437 hospital pharmacy professionals surveyed in January–February 2026 still manage shortages with spreadsheets, 16.5% use dedicated software, and ShortageCheck already sells prediction plus inventory-specific planning, including stock-checking therapeutic alternatives. Vizient already sells supply assurance rather than memos. A public MCP already does the class-plus-shortage loop. MaaTRx's 2026 university story already says "safe alternatives." — [Bluesight release](https://bluesight.com/news/bluesight-releases-2026-hospital-pharmacy-and-privacy-reports/); [Bluesight guide](https://bluesight.com/news/drug-shortage-management-guide/); [Vizient product page](https://www.vizient.com/products/pharmacy/drug-shortages); [rx-shortage-mcp](https://github.com/Travis-Clement-Dev/rx-shortage-mcp); [MaaTRx](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-builds-early-warning-system-drug-shortages)
- The labor buyer is real if stated carefully: Vizient's June 17, 2025 release attributes roughly 20 million hours and nearly $900 million in 2023 labor to managing shortages, versus just under $360 million in the 2019 survey, from 132 respondents reporting on calendar 2023. That supports a drafting tool only as a cut of pharmacist time, not as proof that hospitals will buy another tracker. — [Vizient release](https://www.vizient.com/newsroom/news-releases/new-vizient-survey-finds-drug-shortages-cost-hospitals-nearly-900m-annually-in-labor-expenses)

### Inferences

- Improvement that fixes the demo-drug weakness: keep dopamine, but pin the record to Hikma NDC 0143-9254-25 or 0143-9252-25 and make the first screen the other 40 mg/mL presentations and their availability, dates, and reasons. Title that block "same ingredient, other reported supply," not "substitute." Do not name a different drug. If the October 2026 resupply line is still on the record during the demo, show it. That is the pharmacist's first move, and every field was returned live.
- Improvement that fixes the 1,141 weakness: say 270 current records are Unavailable and 129 are Limited Availability, as of the 2026-10-16 extract, and that ASHP's drug-level count was 227 in the June 2026 series, described there as the second quarterly rise in a row, not the third. Never add those numbers together.
- Improvement that fixes the price weakness: for this NDC, print "no NADAC row" as the price result. Do not invent a price, do not fall back to a web snippet, and do not switch the demo drug unless a live NADAC hit is shown. A missing price is a better demo of judgment than a fake one, because hospital injectables are a poor fit for a retail acquisition-cost file.
- Improvement that fixes the class-list weakness: if RxClass is called, show the class name and then refuse to treat it as candidates. The safety design a judge can see is a hard stop when the class is an EPC as broad as Catecholamine, plus a second shortage check on any row that is displayed, plus the openFDA and NLM sentences on the memo.
- Improvement that fixes the legal weakness: the user is a health-system pharmacist or P&T secretary who already owns the protocol. The artifact is a draft packet for signature. The signature block says the pharmacist determines whether any change is a same-ingredient purchase or a therapeutic interchange that the committee has already authorized. The VA file is not used as the hospital formulary.
- Improvement that fixes the "wrapper" weakness: the memo's only original work is the join that was proved (openFDA to RxNav SCD to DailyMed setid) and the explicit non-conclusion. Do not spend the demo on a chatbot summary of the FDA page. ASHP HTML is a bad dependency after HTTP 403.
- Sponsor use that is load-bearing: the spreadsheet majority in the Bluesight survey, whose time Vizient priced, needs a packet they can file. Bluesight and Vizient already sell the prediction and the physical supply. The unclaimed slice, still unverified as empty, is the cited draft a human signs. That is the only wedge these sources support. It is not a wedge if the draft asserts clinical safety.
- Ways this specific idea loses. Crowded space: a judge who knows ShortageCheck, Vizient Reserve, or the public MCP will not hear "nobody." Clinical-claim risk: "safe substitute" contradicts openFDA and collapses generic substitution into therapeutic interchange. Data that does not join: NADAC miss, Orange Book discontinued rows, Catecholamine EPC, VA page with no drug row. Five-day impossibility sits in the unproven half (a real therapeutic recommendation with citations and a price), not in the sourcing packet, which the live calls already support. Wrapper risk: if the demo treats status=Current as "needs a substitute," it is wrong on the proposed drug, where same-strength product is marked Available.

### Gaps

- No buyer interview and no P&T memo template from a hospital were opened, so the claim that a signed packet is the scarce artifact is an inference from what competitors' pages do not claim.
- Whether any unopened vendor (Lexidrug, Medi-Span, UpToDate, McKesson, Oracle Health, Epic Willow) already emits that packet is unknown.
- A shortage NDC that does return a current NADAC price was not identified, so a priced demo is not yet shown to be possible.
