# Alternative idea for the Open Agent Hackathon 2026

Research date: 2026-10-20. Build window cited by the event page: 22–27 October 2026. Judges named on the event page include Michael Hay, CTO, Zetaris. Sponsors named by NCSA: NVIDIA, SpaceXAI (with Cursor), Bright Data, Zetaris, Meterless. This note recommends one idea only. It is not a drug-shortage pharmacist agent.

## What single idea survives the evidence tests?

### Takeaway

**Modlock** is a bid-day General Decision lock packet for one live Army construction solicitation. A prevailing-wage specialist, not a chatbot, uses it to show which Davis-Bacon decision and modification governs each construction type, to cite base wage and fringe to a line in that decision, and to draft SF-1444 only for a classification the decision does not list. The packet is a draft for the contractor’s representative to sign. The Wage and Hour Division, not the model, approves any added classification.

The idea is ready only as that packet. It is not ready if the demo is a county wage lookup, a services labor-category pricer, or a certified-payroll generator. Those three are already products.

### Inferences

- The person who does this by hand is the contractor’s prevailing-wage specialist or project estimator (eBacon’s own pages use “Prevailing Wage Specialist” as a user). The artifact is not “insights.” It is (1) a lock sheet naming the solicitation, the General Decision numbers, the modification, the construction type, and the county, with each classification’s base and fringe pointed at that decision, and (2) a draft SF-1444, blocks 3–15, only if a needed classification is absent. Block 16 and the contracting-officer section stay blank for humans. FAR 22.406-3 makes DOL the decision-maker. The hackathon demo must say that on the slide.
- W5168W26RA031 is a negotiated RFP (proposals, LPTA, PIEE), not a sealed bid. The 10-day rule in FAR 22.404-6(b) does not govern this record. The rule that does is FAR 22.404-6(c): a modification published on SAM.gov before award is effective. The demo should say that in one sentence and keep the sealed-bid 10-day test as a second, clearly labeled mode. Applying the 10-day rule to Fort Benning would be a factual error.
- The three-decision attachment is the actual join. Heavy, building, and highway rates in the HigherGov summary are not interchangeable (a highway flagger line is not an electrician line). The packet’s job is to stop the estimator from pricing Fort Benning electrical work off the wrong decision. That is a real use, and it is why a single county dropdown product does not already do it.
- Demand that can be cited without inventing a Davis-Bacon-only total: FY2025 government-contract back wages of $64,474,403 across 1,045 concluded actions, on top of a statutory threshold of $2,000 and a form (SF-1444) whose own burden estimate ignores the lookup. One live buyer-facing deadline is October 22, 2026, for W5168W26RA031. That is this week, not a 2027 planning date. The hackathon starts the day that deadline hits, so the team has to archive Attachment 3 on October 20 or 21.
- Data actually touched in this session, three or more endpoints: (1) HigherGov HTML for the notice and the attachment summary, because SAM and the PDF wall did not yield the file; (2) FAR 22.404-6 and FAR 22.406-3 on acquisition.gov, HTML; (3) the SF-1444 PDF on gsa.gov, opened page by page; (4) the WHD government-contracts chart, HTML tables; (5) sam.gov wage-determination URLs, which answered with an empty shell. The shell, plus PIEE as a second portal and HigherGov’s view limit on the 0.16 MB PDF, is the Bright Data reason. It is not LinkedIn and not news. An official opportunities API may exist for notice metadata; it was not shown to return wage-determination rate lines.
- Every material sentence in the packet can point at a source: solicitation fields at the HigherGov notice (and, after download, the SAM notice), decision identity and rates at Attachment 3, timing rule at FAR 22.404-6(c), missing-classification request at SF-1444 block 13 and FAR 22.406-3. If a rate cannot be pointed at a line in the downloaded PDF, it does not go in the packet. The HigherGov summary rates must not be hardcoded.
- Local model: not a requirement. No statute opened here says bid math must stay on premises. The practical reason is real and narrow: Exhibit D/E/F on this solicitation are coefficient workbooks, and a filled workbook plus crew rates is the bid. Joining those to the public decisions on a public API leaks the bid. The public-only demo (notice + attachment + FAR + blank SF-1444) does not need a local model. NVIDIA is optional, not load-bearing. Do not claim a regulatory on-prem mandate.
- Sponsor mapping that is load-bearing: Bright Data or an equivalent browser fetch, because plain HTTP to the wage-determination URL returned no document and the attachment sat behind a second site’s view limit and a PIEE link. Zetaris or any virtualization layer, because the packet is a join of the notice, three decisions inside one PDF, FAR 22.404-6(c), and SF-1444 fields, with an optional on-prem rate sheet. Cursor is how the team builds, not a reason the product exists. Meterless and SpaceXAI were not verified against product pages and are not part of the claim.
- Five-day slice: one notice, W5168W26RA031. Download Attachment 3. Emit a lock sheet that separates GA20260297, GA20260320, and GA20260069, states that FAR 22.404-6(c) applies because the notice is negotiated, and quotes only classifications read from the PDF, each with decision number and modification. If the user types one craft that is absent from the heavy decision, fill SF-1444 blocks 11–13 as a draft and leave signatures blank. Do not build certified payroll, coefficient pricing, subcontractor flow-down, a SAM monitor, a national wage database, or submission to DOL.
- How it loses: the demo becomes a county rate lookup (scawages already prices SCA bids from a SAM crawl); it becomes a services pricing workbook (GovDash Pricer already extracts wage-determination details); it emits a WH-347 (LCPtracker, eBacon, and FOUNDATION already do that); it quotes HigherGov’s summary instead of the PDF; it applies the sealed-bid 10-day rule to this RFP; or it states a legal wage rather than a draft for the contractor to sign. The space around “prevailing wage software” is crowded. The unoccupied slice is the solicitation-specific three-decision lock sheet plus SF-1444 draft. If the team cannot show that difference in three minutes, they should not submit this.

## Which two near-misses were second, and why?

### Takeaway

The Lead and Copper Rule Improvements replacement plan and the PFAS drinking-water initial-monitoring memo are real packets with real deadlines, and both lose to products or to a missing live row. They are runners-up, not the recommendation.

### Cited Findings

- LCRI runner-up. A service line replacement plan is required for any water system with at least one lead, galvanized-requiring-replacement, or unknown service line. It is due to the state by November 1, 2027, must be publicly accessible, and for systems serving more than 50,000 people must be posted online. The same date is the due date for the baseline inventory. EPA’s October 2024 fact sheet lists required contents, including a strategy to identify unknown materials. — [EPA LCRI service-line inventory fact sheet (PDF)](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-10/final_lcri_fact-sheet_service-line-inventory.pdf); [EPA LCRI state and water-system fact sheet (PDF)](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-10/final_lcri_fact-sheet_statespws.pdf)
- EPA’s November 25, 2025 news release, retrieved via search (a later direct fetch of the same URL failed), says EPA is distributing $3 billion in new Drinking Water State Revolving Fund lead-service-line assistance and reallocating $1.1 billion, and that the updated inventory estimate is 4 million lead service lines, down from 9 million. The public service-line dashboard repeats the 4 million estimate and says about 3 million are reported lead plus galvanized-requiring-replacement, with another estimated 1 million among unknowns. — [EPA news release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-announces-3-billion-new-funding-states-reduce-lead-drinking-water); [SDWIS service-line inventory report](https://ordspub.epa.gov/ords/sfdw_rest/r/sfdw/sdwis_fed_reports_public/service-line-inventory)
- A March 16, 2026 trade article says utilities scrambled to finish initial inventories in 2024, some consultants declined work, and the replacement plan is one of several items due November 1, 2027. That is industry commentary, not a measured hour count. — [Water Finance & Management](https://waterfm.com/from-inventory-to-action-countdown-to-the-2027-lcri-deadline/)
- What killed LCRI as the winner: 120Water’s LCRI page says its software covers inventory, verification, replacement, sampling, notifications, and reporting, and its timeline lists the November 1, 2027 replacement-plan submission. BlueConduit’s January 28, 2025 launch of “LSL Replacements” says the tool generates LCRI-compliant replacement prioritization inside Esri, including impacted populations and the Environmental Justice Index, and its FAQ says that prioritization is a critical component of the required plan. The plan artifact is already a product category. — [120Water LCRI](https://www.120water.com/lcr-faq); [BlueConduit LSL Replacements launch](https://blueconduit.com/post/blueconduit-launches-lsl-replacements-tool/); [BlueConduit LSL Replacements FAQ](https://blueconduit.com/lsl-replacements/)
- PFAS runner-up. 40 CFR 141.900 and the EPA quick reference guides set April 26, 2027 as the date initial monitoring must be reported to the primacy agency and compliance monitoring begins. MCL compliance is April 26, 2029. Surface-water systems and large groundwater systems must take four samples, 2 to 4 months apart, within 12 months, at each entry point. A state may accept data collected on or after January 1, 2019 with Methods 533 or 537.1. EPA’s November 21, 2024 memo to primacy agencies says large groundwater systems that only have two UCMR 5 samples must collect two more. — [eCFR 40 CFR 141.902](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/141.902); [EPA initial-monitoring quick reference (PDF)](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPDF.cgi/P101CY4U.PDF?Dockey=P101CY4U.PDF); [EPA November 2024 monitoring memo (PDF)](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-11/memorandum-on-final-pfas-npdwr-monitoring-and-reporting_wdds-and-primacy-agencies.pdf)
- UCMR 5 occurrence data are public. EPA’s data-finder page says the final UCMR 5 dataset was released in August 2026 and that UCMR 5 results do not themselves determine MCL compliance. — [EPA UCMR 5 Data Finder](https://www.epa.gov/dwucmr/fifth-unregulated-contaminant-monitoring-rule-data-finder)
- What made PFAS second: the memo a compliance coordinator would write (which entry points, which historical samples count, which extra samples are still due before April 26, 2027) is more specific than a generic summary, and the calendar is tighter than November 2027. It is not the winner because no single PWSID with sample rows was opened in this session, 120Water already sells sampling-program management, and EPA already operates the Compliance Monitoring Data Portal path described in the monitoring guides. Building the memo without a live row would have been a mocked demo.

### Inferences

- LCRI is the better-known water idea and the one a Zetaris judge is more likely to have seen beside other environmental joins. It fails the competitive test even though the deadline, the dollar figure, and the named artifact are real.
- PFAS would become the winner only after someone opens one system’s UCMR 5 sample rows and shows that 120Water does not emit the grandfathering worksheet. That was not shown here.

### Gaps

- The EPA November 25, 2025 news URL failed on a direct fetch after the search extract. Treat the $3 billion and 4 million figures as search-retrieved page text, not as a second successful fetch.
- No PWSID, entry-point identifier, or sample row was opened for PFAS. No state inventory spreadsheet row was opened for LCRI.

## Which directions were rejected, and what killed them?

### Takeaway

Price-transparency files, sanctions screening, generic grant or proposal writing, clinical decision support, certified payroll itself, prior-authorization APIs, and the TSCA PFAS manufacturing report were rejected. Each one is either already a product category, medically or legally autonomous, or not a stable deadline.

### Cited Findings

- Hospital and insurer price transparency was not re-litigated. It is a named product category the assignment said to reject on contact. GovDash and Sweetspot already occupy “AI reads the solicitation and drafts the proposal,” which is the generic-proposal version of the same failure. Sweetspot’s own workflow page is discovery, compliance matrices, and proposal drafts, not a wage lock sheet. — [Sweetspot GovCon workflows](https://sweetspot.so/govcon-workflows/); [GovDash Pricer](https://www.govdash.com/pricer)

CMS’s fact sheet and a 2026 implementation-vendor page both describe a crowded FHIR build (CRD, DTR, PAS, Inferno). That is health-IT implementation and sits next to clinical decision support. It is not a packet a person still assembles from three public files. — [CMS CMS-0057-F fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-and-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f); [CMS API standards page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/implementation-guides-and-standards/application-programming-interfaces-apis-and-relevant-standards-and-implementation-guides-igs)

Secondary pages still quoting an October 13, 2026 close are stale relative to that April rule. Chemical-regulatory content tools already exist around this report, and the scope is mid-revision. — [EPA TSCA PFAS reporting update](https://www.epa.gov/chemicals-under-tsca/update-reporting-deadline-tsca-pfas-reporting-rule); [public-inspection PDF](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-07062.pdf)

They are the categories the assignment said to drop, and the GovDash/Sweetspot pages already show the proposal-writing variant is staffed. No dosing, coverage, or payment decision is recommended anywhere in this note.
- FERC/PJM interconnection, EDGAR filings, and NOAA-heavy environmental joins were not pursued, because the assignment said a Zetaris judge may already have seen PUDL, EDGAR, and NOAA cookbooks. No energy-generation or weather demo is recommended.
- SCA wage pricing as a standalone idea is killed by scawages.com, opened above. The surviving idea uses a Davis-Bacon construction attachment with three decisions, which that product does not claim.

### Inferences

- The competitive kill on Modlock is partial, not total. Payroll and SCA pricing and services proposal pricing are taken. The Fort Benning lock sheet is not, on the pages opened.
- A team that “adds AI” to certified payroll, prior authorization, lead-pipe inventory, or proposal writing is entering a category these sources already occupy.

### Gaps

- No separate product page was opened for Turquoise, ComplyAdvantage, or UpToDate. Those category rejections rest on the assignment’s named categories plus the adjacent products that were opened (GovDash, Sweetspot, 120Water, BlueConduit, scawages, LCPtracker).
- SpaceXAI and Meterless were not checked, so they are not reasons to prefer or reject any idea above.
